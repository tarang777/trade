# Research notes: daily life in Pune

Purpose: story material and accuracy guardrails. ⚠️ = verify before publishing (it changes, or the sources conflict). 🙂 = a cultural stereotype: use it affectionately and don't present it as hard fact.

## A. The city in one paragraph
Pune is Maharashtra's second-largest city. It is a historic Maratha/Peshwa seat (Shaniwar Wada), long called the **"Oxford of the East"** for its colleges, and it is now a major **IT and auto-manufacturing hub** (Hinjewadi, Kharadi, Magarpatta, Chakan, Pimpri-Chinchwad). Two cities live side by side: **old Pune** (the Peths: Sadashiv, Narayan, Shaniwar, Kasba, with narrow lanes, wadas and old families) and **new Pune** (Baner, Balewadi, Wakad, Kharadi: glass towers, gated societies, migrants from all over India).

## C. Signature themes (episode fuel)
1. **Puneri Patya** 🙂: famous sarcastic and witty signboards on gates and shops ("No parking. Even if you're the Prime Minister." style). Perfect for visual gags. *Write original signs; don't copy real photos.*
2. **The siesta / 1–4 pm closures** 🙂: a beloved meme, partly true in old Pune and much less so in malls and new Pune.
3. **Two-wheeler city**: Pune has very high two-wheeler ownership. Reporting says Pune's vehicle registrations have crossed its population, and it ranks among India's slowest-traffic cities in the TomTom index ⚠️ (re-verify the ranking year) [1][2].
4. **PMPML buses**: a large bus network (the fleet is reported in the low thousands), often crowded at peak hours ⚠️ ridership figures vary by source [3][4].
5. **Pune Metro**: Purple Line (PCMC ↔ Swargate) and Aqua Line (Vanaz ↔ Ramwadi) are operating. The **Hinjewadi–Shivajinagar Line 3** has been in the final testing and opening stages through 2026, and reports conflict ⚠️ **check its status on the publish date** [5][6][7].
6. **Food**: misal pav (spice levels, "tarri" gravy), vada pav, poha, sabudana khichdi (fasting days), bakarwadi, Mastani (a thick milkshake, a Pune speciality), bun maska and chai, kanda bhaji in the rain, pithla-bhakri at Sinhagad. *Avoid naming specific shops in visuals.*
7. **Monsoon (June–September)**: the first rain is an event. Sinhagad, Lonavala and Tamhini ghat weekend drives; waterfalls; potholes and flooded underpasses (a light-touch gag).
8. **Ganeshotsav (August–September)**: started as a public festival by Lokmanya Tilak in the 1890s. Neighbourhood mandals, **dhol-tasha pathaks** (drum troupes that practise for months, including many young professionals and students), immersion processions. Treat it with respect.
9. **Students and the "Oxford of the East"**: FC Road, hostels, the "Pune is a city of students and pensioners" joke 🙂.
10. **Pensioners' paradise** 🙂: the pleasant weather (historically), morning walks, laughing clubs.
11. **Language**: Marathi with a famously "pure" Puneri accent 🙂, plus Hindi and English in the IT belt. Code-switching is the norm in new Pune.
12. **Old vs. New**: wadas vs. glass towers, cycles (Pune was once India's "cycle city") vs. cabs.

## E. Sensitivity notes
- No caste, community, or Marathi-vs-non-Marathi conflict humour.
- Festivals: show devotion and joy. Comedy comes from people and logistics (crowds, practice noise), never from the deity or the rituals.
- Use "Pune" as the city name throughout.
